Nile Health Network

A bilingual nonprofit website concept supporting awareness and engagement around the crisis in Sudan.

CLASS PROJECT · CONCEPT PROTOTYPE · NOT LAUNCHED
ENGLISH / العربية

Website Prototype

Nile Health Network is a fictional organization I created for a class project. Program, sponsor, and impact content in the prototype demonstrates the proposed experience.

Project overview

I developed a website concept to help visitors learn about Sudan, understand the organization’s proposed mission, and find ways to get involved.

As the daughter of Sudanese immigrants, this project was personal. The conflict has affected my family, and I wanted to use my design skills to bring attention to a cause that connects to my own life.

Focus
UX research and interface design
Methods
Surveys, interviews, affinity mapping, journey mapping, and competitor analysis
My role
Research planning and analysis, website structure, interface design, English and Arabic layouts, and mobile adaptations

Who I designed for

I focused on people with personal ties to Sudan, including members of the diaspora and people with family affected by the conflict, alongside supporters learning about the crisis for the first time.

01 / THE CHALLENGE

Challenge

The site needed to communicate urgency while giving visitors room to understand the cause. I wanted the experience to be direct, easy to navigate, and respectful of the people represented.

How might we help English- and Arabic-speaking visitors understand the mission and find a clear way to get involved?
02 / USER RESEARCH

Research

I collected 10 survey responses and conducted 3 interviews, combining quantitative responses with descriptions of people’s experiences. I organized the responses through affinity mapping to identify recurring themes.

I asked about previous donations, nonprofit website habits, causes participants supported, how they discovered organizations, and what made them confident enough to get involved. I also asked about awareness of the crisis in Sudan and the importance of multilingual content.

TRUST

Donation Confidence

Questions explored what evidence participants wanted before donating or volunteering.

CONNECTION

Stories and Connection

I asked which stories, images, and messages helped people connect with an organization.

ACCESS

Language and Awareness

I explored language preferences and how much visitors already knew about Sudan.

This was a small exploratory sample. I used the patterns to guide the concept, rather than treating them as representative of all potential donors.

03 / KEY FINDINGS

Findings

01

Transparency and Impact

Participants cared about impact and transparency. They wanted to understand how support would reach people, rather than seeing broad promises.

02

Personal Connection

Personal stories and a connection to the cause stood out. Social media was also a common way participants discovered organizations.

03

Awareness and Language

Some participants knew about the crisis but had only a surface-level understanding. Multilingual content also mattered to several respondents.

04 / FROM RESEARCH TO DESIGN

Design Decisions

01

Homepage

Because visitors might arrive from social media, I put the mission and a direct action on the homepage. People should not need to explore several pages to understand what the organization is proposing.

02

Transparency

I included areas for proposed programs, stories, and impact information. These explore how an organization could explain its work more specifically and make its activities easier to understand.

03

Content Structure

The experience guides visitors from learning about Sudan to understanding the mission, then exploring ways to help. I aimed to inform without overwhelming visitors or relying only on distressing imagery.

04

English and Arabic

Feedback about multilingual content informed the language switcher. As an Arabic speaker, I especially enjoyed adjusting translated titles and layouts. It made clear that supporting another language means more than replacing words.

05

Visual Design

I chose white, dark blue, and light blue, with clear headings and space between sections. I wanted the emotional weight of the content to come through without making the interface feel chaotic.

05 / MOBILE DESIGN

Mobile Design

I started with a laptop layout. Moving to mobile meant stacking grids, resizing images, and rearranging sections that became crowded on a smaller screen.

That was one of the most challenging parts of the project. Next time, I would work on desktop and mobile together and test on phones earlier, instead of treating responsiveness as a final adjustment.

06 / REFLECTION

Reflection

This project pushed me to think about how I represent a real crisis. I wanted to raise awareness without reducing Sudanese people to statistics or distressing images.

Designing the Arabic version was especially meaningful. Working through translated titles and layout changes helped me connect inclusion to concrete design decisions.

What I would do next

I would test the prototype in both languages, especially on mobile. Tasks would include explaining the mission, finding program information, switching languages, and locating a way to get involved.

The result is a bilingual concept prototype. I have not measured donation conversion or demonstrated that it improves trust; those are questions for future testing.
